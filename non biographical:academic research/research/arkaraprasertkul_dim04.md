Non Arkaraprasertkul's citation profile reveals a scholar operating in a low-citation-density interdisciplinary field (architecture/urban studies/anthropology) with **sustained above-median impact** relative to field norms. With 469 total citations over ~18 years of scholarly activity (first publication 2007), an h-index of 12, and 49% of all citations accrued since 2021, the profile indicates **strong recent relevance** and a **resilient citation trajectory** despite the inherent challenges of interdisciplinary, practitioner-oriented scholarship. ### 1.2 Pattern Interpretation

The citation trajectory exhibits a **two-peaked pattern with irregular year-to-year fluctuations**, which research on citation trajectories identifies as the most common pattern across both highly cited and least cited scholars [^12^]. Weinberger et al. found that "the most common pattern in both groups of scholars was two-peaked" and that "the most cited scholars had a higher chance to match profiles that reflect early-career success" [^12^].

**Key observations from the pattern:**

1. **The 2020-2021 dip**: The decline from 14 (2019) to 6 (2020) and 4 (2021) likely reflects pandemic-era citation delays, a phenomenon widely documented during COVID-19 when editorial delays slowed publication cycles across all disciplines [^21^].

2. **The 2022 spike (19 citations)**: This represents a **375% increase** from 2021. Such spikes typically occur when foundational publications mature into their peak citation window. Davis and Cochran's analysis of cited half-lives shows that "as journals attracted more citations, a larger proportion of them were directed toward older papers," indicating a structural shift toward citing established work [^64^]. For urban studies specifically, the cited half-life is **7.46 years** [^64^], meaning papers published ~7-8 years prior (circa 2014-2015) would be entering their peak citation period around 2022. This spike is also consistent with the trajectory pattern described by Powers et al. of "second period peakers" who experience renewed citation growth in mid-career [^12^].

3. **2024-2025 recovery**: The rebound to 11 (2024) and 16 (2025) suggests the scholar's newer work on smart cities and digital urbanism is gaining traction, potentially reflecting interdisciplinary expansion into higher-citation subfields.

## 2. The "Since 2021" Metric Analysis (229/469 = ~49%)

### 2.1 Significance Assessment

Having **49% of all lifetime citations accrue in the most recent 5-year window** is a **strong indicator of recent impact vitality**. This metric must be interpreted through multiple lenses:

**a) Relative to typical academic careers:**

For most scholars, citation accumulation follows a cumulative, ever-growing curve where earlier work continues to be cited while new work adds fresh citations. Having nearly half of all citations in the last 5 years of an ~18-year career suggests that **recent publications are performing disproportionately well** or that older work is experiencing renewed attention.

The correlation between past and future citations in social sciences is moderate (Spearman's rho = 0.584 for mid-age researchers) [^14^], meaning past citations are a reasonably good but not perfect predictor of future citations. The fact that 49% of citations are recent suggests **strong momentum** that exceeds what correlation-based predictions would forecast.

**b) The h5-index context:**

Google Scholar provides a 5-year h-index (h5-index) specifically to track recent impact [^87^]. The ratio of "since 2021" citations (229) to total citations (469) at 49% is significantly higher than the ratio one would expect from a purely cumulative model. For a scholar with an 18-year career, if citations were evenly distributed, only ~28% (5/18) would fall in the most recent 5-year window. The actual figure of **49% is 1.75x higher than this baseline**, indicating recent work is substantially outperforming historical averages.

**c) Comparison to urban planning faculty norms:**

According to ScholarMetrics data tracking ~1,057 urban planning faculty in the U.S. and Canada, the median total citations for assistant professors is 197, for associate professors is 675, and for full professors is 1,971 [^78^]. With 469 total citations, Non Arkaraprasertkul falls between the assistant and associate professor medians. However, the critical context is that **these benchmarks are for tenure-track faculty positions**, which represent a fundamentally different career model.

Hirsch originally suggested that **m >= 1.0 indicates a successful scientist**, m >= 2.0 indicates an outstanding scientist, and m >= 3.0 indicates a truly exceptional career [^20^]. However, these benchmarks are highly field-dependent.

## 5. Career Trajectory Classification

### 5.1 Trajectory Pattern Analysis

Based on the citation data and career history, Non Arkaraprasertkul's trajectory most closely aligns with the **"busy case"** pattern identified by Feichtinger et al. [^12^]: "slow start followed by an accelerated research intensity." This pattern is characterized by:

- Early foundational work (2007-2011): Building base on Shanghai urbanism
- Mid-career expansion (2012-2018): Major publications, peak citation growth
- Recent period (2019-2025): Continued productivity with interdisciplinary expansion into smart cities/digital urbanism

The Powers et al. framework classifies scholars as "first period peakers," "second period peakers," "mid-career increasers," etc. [^12^]. The 2022 spike at ~15 years post-first-publication suggests a **"second period peak"** pattern, where citation growth accelerates in mid-career as foundational work matures and new interdisciplinary directions gain audience.

### 5.2 Comparison to Practitioner-Scholar Trajectories

Non Arkaraprasertkul's career diverges significantly from the standard tenure-track model. His profile as "Senior Expert in Smart City Promotion" at Thailand's Digital Economy Promotion Agency [^44^] places him in the **"alt-ac" (alternative academic)** category. Research on alt-ac careers notes that "the career path of the alternative academic is less defined than that of their faculty counterparts" and "the meaning of scholarship also differs between traditional and alternative academics" [^112^].

For practitioner-scholars, traditional citation metrics may understate impact because:
1. Research is often published in practitioner-oriented venues
2. Impact may manifest in policy documents rather than academic citations
3. Books and book chapters — common in architecture/anthropology — are less consistently captured in citation databases

> **Claim**: Alt-ac careers lack clear milestones and their scholarship may be undervalued by traditional metrics.

## 8. The 2022 Spike: Detailed Investigation

### 8.1 Possible Contributing Factors

The 2022 spike to 19 citations (from 4 in 2021) can be explained through multiple non-mutually-exclusive mechanisms:

**a) Maturation of foundational work**: Papers published ~2014-2015 ( Shanghai urbanism work) would be ~7-8 years old in 2022, precisely at the cited half-life peak for urban studies (7.46 years) [^64^].

**b) Google Scholar coverage dynamics**: Google Scholar citation counts can fluctuate due to index coverage changes. Martin-Martin et al. documented cases where "Google Scholar was only able to find 60% of all Web of Science citations" in certain years due to temporary coverage drops [^21^]. However, the 2022 spike in this profile is followed by continued elevated counts in 2024-2025, suggesting it is not merely a data artifact.

**c) Interdisciplinary spillover**: The scholar's transition into smart cities and digital urbanism work may have exposed earlier anthropological/architectural work to new audiences in computer science and policy fields with different citation practices.

**d) Two-peak trajectory pattern**: As noted in trajectory research, "the most common pattern of the citation trajectories among Nobel Prize laureates in economics manifested a peak around the time of the Nobel Prize followed by steady decline" [^12^]. While not suggesting Nobel-level impact, the pattern of a mid-career citation revival is well-documented.
