## Positioning of Dr. Non Arkaraprasertkul in Shanghai Urban Studies, Gentrification Research, and Urban Anthropology

**Prepared:** 2025  
**Subject:** Non Arkaraprasertkul (Harvard University, Department of Anthropology)  
**Focus:** Competitive landscape analysis across three overlapping fields

---

## Executive Summary

Dr. Non Arkaraprasertkul occupies a distinctive interdisciplinary niche at the intersection of urban anthropology, architectural heritage studies, and gentrification research focused specifically on Shanghai. His positioning is characterized by: (1) a rare combination of architectural training and ethnographic methodology; (2) deep specialization in Shanghai's lilong (alleyway) neighborhoods; (3) publications in both top-tier urban studies venues (Urban Studies) and leading heritage journals (International Journal of Heritage Studies); and (4) an intellectual lineage connecting Harvard anthropology (Michael Herzfeld advisor) with MIT architecture. When benchmarked against leading scholars in the three reference fields—gentrification studies (Lees h-index 55, Slater h-index 36, Wyly cited ~13,000), China urban studies (Fulong Wu h-index 66), and urban anthropology (Holston, Low)—Arkaraprasertkul operates at a significantly earlier career stage with proportionally lower but field-appropriate citation metrics. His unique value proposition lies in bridging ethnographic methods with urban spatial analysis, a combination rare among both urban studies and anthropology scholars.

## 5. Role of Ethnography in Contemporary Urban Studies

Ethnography is increasingly recognized as a critical methodology in urban studies, particularly for understanding lived experiences of urban transformation. The 2024 "Doing Mobile Ethnography" workshop at Tongji University in Shanghai explicitly addressed "how does one conduct ethnography in/across the rapidly shifting South urban spaces?" [^211^][^316^].

The textbook *Introducing Urban Anthropology* (Jaffe & de Koning, 2021) identifies ethnography as central to understanding urban processes: "anthropologists such as Teresa Caldeira (2000) and Setha Low (2003) have done ethnographic research in gated communities and condominiums" [^264^]. The text also notes that "in the 1990s, anthropologists increasingly began to see consumption rather than production as the main characteristic of the late modern city" [^264^].

Arkaraprasertkul's methodological distinctiveness lies in his application of ethnographic methods (participant observation, narrative analysis) to architectural heritage questions. His 2016 dissertation abstract explicitly states: "I use ethnography to explore the complex processes of urbanization and globalization in Shanghai" [^259^].

> **Evidence Claim:** Ethnography is increasingly valued in urban studies for Global South research, as demonstrated by the 2024 Tongji University workshop on mobile ethnography.  
> **Source:** Urban Studies Foundation / Asia Africa Youth  
> **URL:** https://www.urbanstudiesfoundation.org/events/cfp-doing-mobile-ethnography  
> **Date Accessed:** 2024-05-13  
> **Excerpt:** "How does one conduct ethnography in/across the rapidly shifting South urban spaces? How can the mobile youth within the Southern urbanisation be seen/heard?"  
> **Context:** Validates ethnography's growing importance in urban studies methodology  
> **Confidence:** HIGH

## 10. Synthesis: Competitive Positioning Statement

### 10.1 Field Positioning Matrix

| Dimension | Arkaraprasertkul | vs. Gentrification Scholars | vs. China Urban Scholars | vs. Urban Anthropologists |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Geographic focus** | Shanghai | Global/Western-centric | China-wide | Brazil/Costa Rica/etc. |
| **Primary method** | Ethnography + Architecture | Quantitative/critical theory | Quantitative/mixed | Ethnography |
| **Heritage angle** | Central | Peripheral | Absent | Emerging |
| **Career stage** | Early (PhD 2016) | Established | Established | Established |
| **h-index range** | ~6-10 | 36-55 | 30-66 | 20-40 |

### 10.2 Competitive Advantages

1. **Methodological uniqueness:** The combination of architectural training (MIT) + ethnographic method (Harvard) is rare in all three reference fields [^172^]
2. **Deep Shanghai expertise:** Years of fieldwork living in lilong neighborhoods provides granular knowledge unavailable to most China urban scholars [^172^]
3. **Conceptual innovation:** The "heritage gentrification" concept fills a gap between Western gentrification theory and Chinese urban reality [^23^]
4. **Dual-journal publishing:** Publication in both *Urban Studies* and *IJHS* demonstrates cross-field relevance
5. **Intellectual lineage:** Harvard anthropology (Herzfeld) + MIT architecture provides prestigious institutional backing

### 10.3 Competitive Disadvantages

1. **Early career stage:** 9 years post-PhD means substantially lower citation metrics than established scholars
2. **Narrow geographic focus:** Shanghai-only research limits comparative breadth relative to Fulong Wu or Loretta Lees
3. **Limited quantitative methods:** Ethnographic focus may limit engagement with dominant quantitative paradigms in urban studies
4. **Single-author pattern:** Less collaborative than Fulong Wu (frequent co-authors with He, Webster) or gentrification scholars (Lees-Slater-Wyly)
5. **Absence from major rankings:** Not yet listed in Research.com, ScholarGPS, or other major academic rankings

### 10.4 Strategic Positioning

Arkaraprasertkul occupies a **distinctive niche** that does not directly compete with either the large-scale quantitative urban studies of Fulong Wu or the activist-oriented gentrification scholarship of Loretta Lees. Instead, his work complements these traditions by providing the ethnographic depth and architectural specificity that macro-scale analyses often lack. His closest intellectual kin are urban anthropologists like James Holston and Setha Low, though his architectural training gives him a technical vocabulary they lack.

The competitive landscape suggests Arkaraprasertkul's trajectory should focus on:
- Consolidating the "heritage gentrification" conceptual framework
- Expanding comparative work beyond Shanghai
- Building collaborative networks with China urban scholars
- Leveraging his unique architecture-anthropology dual identity
