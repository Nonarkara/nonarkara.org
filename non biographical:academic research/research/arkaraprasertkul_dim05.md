# Dimension 05: Interdisciplinary Research Span Analysis — Non Arkaraprasertkul

## Executive Summary

Non Arkaraprasertkul represents a distinctive **"proximally interdisciplinary"** scholar whose work spans architecture, anthropology, urban studies, heritage studies, and smart city technology. His research profile illustrates both the opportunities and challenges of interdisciplinary scholarship: while his work demonstrates methodological innovation (ethnography in architectural/urban research) and publications across seven distinct disciplinary journal categories, the bibliometric literature suggests such scholars face systematic disadvantages in traditional citation indices and journal rankings. The Google Scholar labels (Smart City, Anthropology, Design) reflect his actual research trajectory, though the "Smart City" dimension represents a more recent career evolution rather than his foundational academic output.

## 1. Interdisciplinary Citation Patterns: The Evidence

### 1.1 The Core Finding: Proximal vs. Distal Interdisciplinarity

The most relevant study for understanding Arkaraprasertkul's citation profile is Yegros-Yegros et al. (2015), which analyzed 62,408 articles across four scientific fields and found that **the relationship between interdisciplinarity and citation impact depends critically on the *type* of interdisciplinarity** [^1^].

**Key Evidence:**

| Claim | Interdisciplinary research exhibits an inverted U-shape relationship with citation impact — moderate interdisciplinarity ("proximal IDR") yields highest citations, while both mono-disciplinary and highly disparate ("distal IDR") work receive fewer citations. |
|-------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Yegros-Yegros, A., Rafols, I., & D'Este, P. (2015). "Does Interdisciplinary Research Lead to Higher Citation Impact? The Different Effect of Proximal and Distal Interdisciplinarity." *PLoS ONE*, 10(10), e0135095. |
| URL | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4534379/ |
| Date | 2015 |
| Excerpt | "Publications that accrue the most citations are moderately interdisciplinary (neither too much, nor too little)... highly cited papers tend to cite various disciplinary categories (higher variety), but cite little outside their disciplinary vicinity (lower disparity) and in small proportions (lower balance). We propose the concepts of **proximal** and **distal interdisciplinarity**... practicing 'meek' or 'shy' (proximal) interdisciplinarity pays off in citations, but that brazen, audacious (distal) interdisciplinary efforts are not rewarded with citation success." |
| Context | Analyzed 62,408 articles in Cellular Biology, Electrical Engineering, Food Science, and Physics using WoS categories, variety/balance/disparity metrics, and 5-year citation windows. |
| Confidence | **HIGH** — Large sample, regression-controlled, peer-reviewed. |

### 1.2 Implications for Arkaraprasertkul

Arkaraprasertkul's work appears to practice **"proximal interdisciplinarity"** — his publications draw from related fields (architecture, urban studies, anthropology, heritage) that are cognitively proximate rather than highly disparate. This positioning should theoretically yield **moderate citation advantages** according to the Yegros-Yegros framework. However, the limitation is that citation windows in the study were 5 years; interdisciplinary work may accrue citations over longer periods [^1^].

### 1.3 The Inverted U-Shape: Confirmed Across Multiple Studies

| Claim | Multiple independent studies confirm that strictly disciplinary research typically receives *lower* citations than moderately interdisciplinary work, but highly interdisciplinary (distal) work also suffers citation penalties. |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Larivière, V., & Gingras, Y. (2010). "On the relationship between interdisciplinary and scientific impact." *Scientometrics*, and subsequent meta-analyses. |
| URL | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4534379/ (citing Larivière & Gingras) |
| Date | 2010/2015 |
| Excerpt | "In all disciplines, highly disciplinary or highly interdisciplinary were associated with a low citation rate, suggesting an inverted U relationship between citation impact and interdisciplinarity." |
| Context | Analysis of all articles in WoS 2000. |
| Confidence | **HIGH** — Widely replicated finding. |

### 1.4 Long-Term vs. Short-Term Citation Dynamics

| Claim | Interdisciplinary research shows different citation patterns over time: variety and disparity have *negative* effects on short-term citations but *positive* effects on long-term citations (13-year windows). |
|-------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Wang, J., Thijs, B., & Glänzel, W. (2015). "Interdisciplinarity and impact: Distinct effects of variety, balance, and disparity." *PLoS ONE*, 10(11), e0127298. (Cited in Yegros-Yegros et al. 2015) |
| URL | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4534379/ |
| Date | 2015 |
| Excerpt | "Variety and disparity have a negative effect on diversity with 3-year windows, they have a positive effect with 13-year windows." |
| Context | Time-window analysis of interdisciplinary citation effects. |
| Confidence | **HIGH** — Important for evaluating Arkaraprasertkul's recent work (post-2015 publications). |

## 2. Challenges and Advantages of Interdisciplinary Research in Citation Metrics

### 2.1 Systematic Bias in Journal Rankings

| Claim | Journal rankings systematically favor mono-disciplinary research and disadvantage interdisciplinary research in research evaluations, creating institutional barriers to IDR careers. |
|-------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Rafols, I., Leydesdorff, L., O'Hare, A., Nightingale, P., & Stirling, A. (2012). "How journal rankings can suppress interdisciplinarity: A comparison between Innovation Studies and Business & Management." *Research Policy*. |
| URL | https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1227 |
| Date | 2011/2012 |
| Excerpt | "ABS journal rankings favor business and management disciplinary approaches—and thus disadvantage IS units by comparison with more traditional BMS... ostensibly 'excellence-based' journal rankings exhibit a systematic bias in favour of mono-disciplinary research." |
| Context | Quantitative comparison of 6 UK research units using ABS rankings, citation analysis, and interdisciplinarity metrics. Found -0.78 correlation between Rao-Stirling diversity and ABS-based performance. |
| Confidence | **HIGH** — First rigorous quantitative demonstration of ranking bias against IDR. |

### 2.2 Career Penalties for Interdisciplinary Researchers

| Claim | Interdisciplinary researchers face reduced career longevity, lower publication productivity, and greater difficulty in promotion/tenure, despite producing work less likely to be citation "flops." |
|-------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Berkes, E., Marion, M., Milojević, S., & Weinberg, B.A. (2024). "Slow convergence: Career impediments to interdisciplinary biomedical research." *PNAS*, 121, e2402646121. |
| URL | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11317606/ |
| Date | 2024 |
| Excerpt | "While half of the most interdisciplinary researchers (top 1%) stop publishing by year 8, it takes more than 20 y for moderately interdisciplinary researchers (10 to 25% and 25 to 75%) to do the same... interdisciplinary researchers may be less likely to have their work positively evaluated by promotion or tenure committees." |
| Context | Analysis of 2.35 million publications by biomedical PhDs, 1970-2013. Found IDR reduces "flop" probability but also reduces career survival. |
| Confidence | **HIGH** — Recent, large-scale, PNAS publication. |

### 2.3 The Citation Advantage for Moderate IDR

| Claim | Moderately interdisciplinary papers have greater citation impact, especially in the long term, although they experience higher variance in outcomes. |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Source | Giorgi, S. & Weber, K. (2025). "Evaluating interdisciplinary research: Disparate outcomes for topic and knowledge base." *PNAS*, 2409752122. |

| Date | 2025 |
| Excerpt | "Moderately interdisciplinary papers have greater citation impact (36), especially in the long term (37), although they experience higher variance." |

### 10.2 Key Conclusions

1. **Arkaraprasertkul exemplifies "proximal interdisciplinarity"** — the type most likely to yield citation advantages according to bibliometric research [^1^]. His fields (architecture, urban studies, anthropology, heritage) are cognitively adjacent rather than highly disparate.

2. **The "Smart City" Google Scholar label reflects career evolution, not original research focus**. His pre-2017 work was overwhelmingly in urban studies, anthropology, and heritage. The smart city dimension represents a post-2017 applied policy turn [^8^][^10^].

3. **His methodological innovation (ethnography in architectural/urban research) is well-positioned** within a growing interdisciplinary field showing 6.29% annual growth [^11^], though the literature notes persistent "epistemic asymmetry" where ethnography remains subordinated to architecture [^11^].

4. **Traditional citation metrics undercount his impact** because: (a) h-index doesn't account for cross-field citation differences [^14^], (b) journal rankings systematically favor mono-disciplinary research [^15^], and (c) his publications are distributed across journals with different citation cultures.

5. **His interdisciplinary credentials are externally validated** through prestigious cross-disciplinary fellowships (Harvard-Yenching, Fulbright, Henry Luce Foundation) and appointments spanning 9 universities in 6 countries across 5 disciplinary categories [^8^].

6. **The career risk typically associated with IDR** (reduced longevity, promotion challenges) [^16^] appears mitigated in his case by transitioning to a policy/practice role (Thailand's depa) where interdisciplinary skills are valued.
